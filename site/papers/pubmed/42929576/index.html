Deciphering Cellular and Spatial Regulatory Identities from Sparse Chromatin Accessibility Landscapes.

Wang, Yunhe; Guo, Songyu; Qian, Jingjing; Liang, Heqiu; Su, Yanchi; Yu, Zhuohan; Li, Xiangtao
Journal of chemical information and modeling · 2026-10-26 · 仅摘要 · 原文

提出CASSIA半监督表示学习框架，从稀疏染色质可及性数据中推断细胞和空间调控身份，涉及染色质可及性建模但未直接建模三维染色质空间架构。

摘要

Chromatin accessibility profiling provides a powerful view of regulatory landscapes across heterogeneous biological systems, enabling the investigation of cell-state diversity and spatially organized regulatory programs. However, deciphering meaningful regulatory identities from sparse and noisy accessibility landscapes remains challenging, particularly for closely related cellular states and neighboring regulatory domains. Unlike transcriptomic measurements, chromatin accessibility does not directly capture canonical marker expression, and regulatory identities must therefore be inferred from indirect evidence, including gene activity scores, transcription factor motif activity, and reference-based annotation. These signals often become ambiguous, limiting the reliability of unsupervised clustering followed by post-hoc interpretation. Here, we present CASSIA (Chromatin Accessibility Semi-Supervised Identity-Aware representation learning), a unified label-guided framework for decoding cellular and spatial regulatory identities from chromatin accessibility landscapes. CASSIA leverages limited high-confidence anchors to learn informative accessibility representations while preserving intrinsic regulatory structures. The framework integrates a binary accessibility denoising autoencoder, a Jensen-Shannon divergence-based clustering constraint, and a similarity difference-aware triplet objective; spatial neighborhood consistency is further incorporated to preserve regulatory organization when spatial information is available. Across diverse chromatin accessibility datasets, CASSIA reconstructs cellular and spatial regulatory identities with improved agreement to biological annotations and tissue organization compared with representative baselines. Identity-associated accessible regions and transcription factor activity profiles reveal distinct regulatory programs underlying inferred identities, demonstrating that CASSIA transforms sparse accessibility landscapes into interpretable cellular and tissue-level regulatory maps.

摘要翻译

染色质可及性图谱为异质性生物系统中的调控景观提供了强有力的视角，使得研究细胞状态多样性和空间组织的调控程序成为可能。然而，从稀疏且嘈杂的可及性景观中解读有意义的调控身份仍然具有挑战性，尤其是对于密切相关的细胞状态和相邻调控域。与转录组测量不同，染色质可及性并不直接捕获经典标志物表达，因此调控身份必须从间接证据中推断，包括基因活性评分、转录因子基序活性和基于参考的注释。这些信号往往变得模糊，限制了无监督聚类及事后解释的可靠性。在此，我们提出CASSIA（染色质可及性半监督身份感知表示学习），一个统一的标签引导框架，用于从染色质可及性景观中解码细胞和空间调控身份。CASSIA利用有限的高置信度锚点来学习信息丰富的可及性表示，同时保留内在调控结构。该框架整合了二元可及性去噪自编码器、基于Jensen-Shannon散度的聚类约束和相似性差异感知三元目标；当空间信息可用时，进一步引入空间邻域一致性以保留调控组织。在多样化的染色质可及性数据集中，与代表性基线相比，CASSIA重建细胞和空间调控身份时与生物学注释和组织组织的一致性更高。身份相关的可及区域和转录因子活性谱揭示了推断身份背后的不同调控程序，表明CASSIA将稀疏的可及性景观转化为可解释的细胞和组织水平调控图谱。

具体问题：如何从稀疏、含噪的染色质可及性景观中，可靠地解码细胞状态与空间调控身份，尤其是对高度相似的细胞状态和相邻调控域。

为什么重要：染色质可及性反映调控景观，可用于研究细胞状态多样性与空间组织调控程序；但不同于转录组，它不直接捕获 canonical marker expression，调控身份只能从间接证据（gene activity scores、TF motif activity、reference-based annotation）推断。

现有方法为何不足：这些间接信号常变得模糊，限制了「无监督聚类 + 事后解释」的可靠性。

精确研究问题（Can ... ?）：Can limited high-confidence anchors guide representation learning to recover cellular and spatial regulatory identities from sparse chromatin accessibility landscapes more reliably than unsupervised clustering followed by post-hoc interpretation?

Reviewer

  • Overall assessment The abstract presents a methodologically plausible framework for a relevant problem in single-cell chromatin accessibility analysis. The combination of semi-supervised learning with multiple objective terms is reasonable, and the inclusion of spatial consistency is timely. However, the abstract provides no quantitative evidence, no dataset descriptions, no baseline specifications, and no evaluation metrics. The core claims of improved performance and interpretability cannot be assessed from the supplied material. The manuscript may have merit, but the case is not established from the abstract alone.
  • Who would be interested in the results, and why Computational biologists and bioinformaticians working on single-cell epigenomics, particularly those developing or applying methods for cell-type annotation, regulatory element identification, and spatial transcriptomics or epigenomics integration. Researchers interested in representation learning for sparse high-dimensional genomic data would also find the methodological contributions relevant.
  • Major strengths The problem addressed is well motivated, as chromatin accessibility data are inherently sparse and regulatory identities are not directly observable. The proposed framework integrates multiple complementary objectives, which is a sensible design. The inclusion of spatial neighborhood consistency is a forward-looking feature given the growing availability of spatial epigenomic data. The abstract is clearly written and accessible to a broad computational biology audience.

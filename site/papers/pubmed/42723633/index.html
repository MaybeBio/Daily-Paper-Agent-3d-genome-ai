Genomic language model for predicting enhancers and their allele-specific activity in the human genome.

Sathian, R; Dutta, P; Ay, F; Davuluri, R V
Bioinformatics (Oxford, England) · 2026-09-11 · 仅摘要 · 原文

该文用DNABERT基因组语言模型预测增强子及其等位基因特异性活性，虽涉及顺式调控元件但未建模染色质三维空间组织或折叠，方法为AI但对象和结构/调控轴均偏弱。

摘要

MOTIVATION: Predicting and deciphering the regulatory logic of enhancers remains a significant challenge due to their complex sequence features and the absence of consistent genetic or epigenetic signatures that distinguish them from other genomic regions. Existing machine learning methods capture nucleotide composition but often fail to model sequence context effectively. RESULTS: We present DNABERT-Enhancer, a novel enhancer prediction method, by applying DNABERT pre-trained language model on the human genome. Using ENCODE registry of candidate cis-regulatory elements (cCREs), we curated a benchmark dataset, consisting of 21,926 enhancers of 201 bp length and 46,159 enhancers of 350 bp length, as positive instances. The best fine-tuned model achieved 88.05% accuracy and a Matthews correlation coefficient of 76% on an independent dataset. Genome-wide application identified 1,684,595 enhancer regions covering 26.65% of the human genome. By performing integrative analyses with DNABERT-based transcription factor models, we identify 2,681 statistically significant loss-of-function and 1,917 gain-of-function enhancer variants, which respectively alter the function of 1,623 and 1,247 ENCODE-cCRE enhancers. Similarly, we identify 4,057 candidate de novo enhancers, created by 5,464 gain-of-function variants. These genome-wide enhancer annotations and candidate genetic variants predicted by DNABERT-Enhancer provide valuable resources for genome interpretation in functional and clinical genomics studies. AVAILABILITY: DNABERT-Enhancer is freely available at https://github.com/DavuluriLab/DNABERT-Enhancer; Trained model predictions can be explored interactively via the web application at https://dnabert-enhancer-datarepo.streamlit.app/. The fine-tuned models are archived and citable through Zenodo (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19157566). SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online.

摘要翻译

动机：由于增强子（enhancer）复杂的序列特征以及缺乏一致的遗传或表观遗传标记来将其与其他基因组区域区分开来，预测和解读增强子的调控逻辑仍然是一项重大挑战。现有的机器学习方法能够捕获核苷酸组成，但通常无法有效建模序列上下文。结果：我们提出了DNABERT-Enhancer，一种新颖的增强子预测方法，通过将DNABERT预训练语言模型应用于人类基因组。利用ENCODE候选顺式调控元件（cCRE）注册表，我们整理了一个基准数据集，包含21,926个长度为201 bp的增强子和46,159个长度为350 bp的增强子作为正例。最佳微调模型在独立数据集上达到了88.05%的准确率和76%的马修斯相关系数（Matthews correlation coefficient）。全基因组应用识别出1,684,595个增强子区域，覆盖人类基因组的26.65%。通过与基于DNABERT的转录因子模型进行整合分析，我们识别出2,681个统计显著的功能丧失型（loss-of-function）和1,917个功能获得型（gain-of-function）增强子变异，这些变异分别改变了1,623个和1,247个ENCODE-cCRE增强子的功能。类似地，我们识别出4,057个候选从头增强子（de novo enhancer），由5,464个功能获得型变异产生。这些由DNABERT-Enhancer预测的全基因组增强子注释和候选遗传变异为功能和临床基因组学研究中的基因组解读提供了宝贵资源。可用性：DNABERT-Enhancer可在https://github.com/DavuluriLab/DNABERT-Enhancer 免费获取；训练模型预测结果可通过网络应用程序https://dnabert-enhancer-datarepo.streamlit.app/ 进行交互式探索。微调模型已存档并通过Zenodo（https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19157566）可引用。补充信息：补充数据可在Bioinformatics在线获取。

02 一句话总结

本文用 DNABERT 预训练基因组语言模型微调，在 ENCODE cCRE 增强子基准上实现 88.05% 准确率（MCC 76%），并通过对 1,684,595 个预测增强子区域做等位基因特异性分析，识别出 2,681 个 loss-of-function 与 1,917 个 gain-of-function 增强子变异及 4,057 个候选 de novo 增强子。

03 研究问题

  • 具体问题：如何从 DNA 序列本身准确预测增强子，并进一步预测增强子活性的等位基因特异性变化（即哪些遗传变异会改变增强子功能）？
  • 为什么重要：增强子缺乏一致的遗传或表观遗传签名，难以与其它基因组区域区分；增强子变异与多种疾病相关，但大规模功能验证成本极高，需要可靠的序列-based 预测工具。
  • 现有方法为何不足：已有机器学习方法主要捕捉核苷酸组成（如 k-mer 频率），未能有效建模序列上下文（sequence context），即调控逻辑依赖的长程、位置依赖的序列特征。
  • 精确研究问题：Can a pre-trained genomic language model (DNABERT) fine-tuned on ENCODE cCRE enhancers achieve higher accuracy in enhancer prediction than composition-based methods, and can its sequence-level predictions be leveraged to identify allele-specific enhancer activity variants at genome scale?

06 核心思想

  1. 表面方法：将 DNABERT（预训练基因组语言模型）在 ENCODE cCRE 增强子基准上微调，做二分类（增强子 vs 非增强子）；随后用微调模型对全基因组扫描，并对每个预测增强子区域内的 SNP 做等位基因特异性评分，区分 loss-of-function 与 gain-of-function 变异。

  2. 核心洞察：预训练语言模型在无标注人类基因组上学习到的序列上下文表征，比任务特定的从头训练模型更有效地捕捉增强子的调控逻辑；且该表征对单核苷酸扰动敏感，使得等位基因特异性预测成为可能。

  3. 可能的普适教训 [Analysis]：基因组语言模型的「上下文敏感性」不仅提升分类性能，还天然支持「扰动分析」——即通过比较参考与替代等位基因的模型输出，可推断变异的功能效应。这一思路可迁移至三维基因组：用语言模型预测 CTCF 结合、拓扑关联域（TAD）边界等，并对边界区域变异做等位基因特异性效应预测。

07 方法总览

  • 输入：DNA 序列（201 bp 或 350 bp 窗口，以候选增强子为中心）
  • 输出：增强子/非增强子二分类概率；变异级等位基因特异性效应（loss/gain-of-function）
  • 模块： 1. 数据构建：从 ENCODE cCRE 注册表提取增强子（21,926 个 201 bp；46,159 个 350 bp）作为正样本，负样本未在摘要中说明（推测为随机基因组区域或非增强子 cCRE） 2. 预训练模型：DNABERT（人类基因组预训练） 3. 微调：在增强子基准上做序列二分类 4. 全基因组扫描：对基因组滑动窗口预测增强子 5. 变异效应分析：对预测增强子区域内的 SNP，比较参考/替代等位基因的模型输出，分类为 loss/gain-of-function
  • 训练：微调细节（学习率、epoch、batch size）未在摘要中提供
  • 工具：DNABERT 框架；代码与模型公开
  • 假设：增强子活性主要由序列决定；DNABERT 的表征对单核苷酸扰动敏感；ENCODE cCRE 是可靠的增强子金标准

文字流程：ENCODE cCRE 增强子 → 构建 201/350 bp 训练窗口 → DNABERT 微调 → 独立测试集评估（88.05% acc, MCC 76%）→ 全基因组扫描 → 对预测增强子内 SNP 做等位基因比较 → 输出功能变异列表（2,681 LOF, 1,917 GOF, 5,464 GOF 变异创建 4,057 个 de novo 增强子）

11 结论正确解读

  • 任务范围：仅限增强子预测与变异效应推断，不涉及启动子、绝缘子、沉默子等其它调控元件。
  • oracle/真值输入：ENCODE cCRE 作为训练金标准；变异效应无实验真值验证。
  • 端到端状态：从序列到增强子注释是端到端的；从序列到变异效应是计算推断，未经功能实验确认。
  • 算力成本：未提供（DNABERT 预训练与全基因组扫描的计算成本未说明）。
  • 历史数据依赖：训练数据来自 ENCODE 项目，其覆盖的细胞类型与实验条件有限。
  • 模型依赖：结果依赖 DNABERT 预训练表征质量；其他语言模型（如 Nucleotide Transformer）未做对比。
  • 最难情形：未报告模型在低复杂度区域、重复序列、或非典型增强子（如无 H3K27ac 标记的增强子）上的表现。
  • 群体/领域边界：仅人类基因组；未测试跨物种迁移。
  • 不确定性：变异效应分类的置信度阈值未提供；de novo 增强子的功能验证缺失。

有边界的复述：DNABERT-Enhancer 在 ENCODE cCRE 增强子基准上达到 88.05% 准确率，并基于序列预测了约 168 万个增强子区域及其中数千个可能改变增强子功能的 SNP；这些预测未经实验验证，且仅适用于人类基因组。

14 Agent 提炼的知识候选

可迁移概念： 1. 预训练语言模型 + 任务微调：DNABERT 的「无标注预训练 → 有标注微调」范式可直接迁移至三维基因组任务，如 TAD 边界预测、CTCF 结合位点预测、启动子-增强子互作预测。 2. 等位基因特异性扰动分析：通过比较参考/替代等位基因的模型输出推断变异效应——这一思路可迁移至三维基因组中的「结构变异对 TAD 结构的影响预测」。 3. 全基因组扫描策略：滑动窗口 + 模型推理 + 阈值筛选的流程可迁移至全基因组染色质状态注释。

可迁移方法： - 用 DNABERT 表征替代 one-hot/k-mer 编码，作为三维基因组预测模型（如 TAD 边界分类器）的输入特征。 - 将「loss/gain-of-function」变异分类框架迁移至「TAD 边界破坏/创建」变异分类。

可迁移实验设计： - 用 ENCODE/GSE 数据构建金标准 → 微调 → 独立测试 → 全基因组应用 → 变异效应分析，这一完整流程可直接复用于三维基因组注释任务。

Reviewer

  • Overall assessment The abstract describes a potentially useful application of a pre-trained genomic language model to enhancer prediction, with a substantial genome-wide annotation resource and variant effect predictions. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient detail to evaluate the validity of the core claims. Key methodological aspects, including dataset construction, model fine-tuning procedures, evaluation design, and the variant effect analysis framework, are not described. The reported performance metrics and genome-wide numbers cannot be independently assessed without access to the underlying methods and validation details. The work may be of interest to the functional genomics and regulatory genomics communities, but the case is not established from the abstract alone.
  • Who would be interested in the results, and why Researchers in regulatory genomics, enhancer biology, and genome interpretation would be interested in a new enhancer prediction tool and the associated genome-wide annotations. Clinically oriented genomics groups might find the variant effect predictions useful for interpreting non-coding variants in disease studies. The computational genomics community would be interested in the application of DNABERT to a regulatory prediction task, particularly the integration with transcription factor binding models.
  • Major strengths The work addresses a real problem in enhancer prediction, namely the need for sequence context modeling beyond nucleotide composition. The use of a pre-trained genomic language model is a reasonable and current approach. The reported scale of genome-wide annotation and variant effect prediction suggests a potentially valuable community resource. The availability of code, a web application, and archived models is commendable for reproducibility.
  • Major Concerns
  • Concern ID R1-M1
  • Severity Major
  • Blocking Yes
  • Axis Technical soundness
  • Claim pointer The model achieved 88.05% accuracy and MCC of 76% on an independent dataset.
  • Evidence pointer Abstract, Results section; location not provided
  • Concern The abstract reports performance on an "independent dataset" but does not describe how this dataset was constructed, how it relates to the training and validation sets, or whether it is truly independent in terms of genomic regions, cell types, or sequence similarity. No information is provided on the negative set construction, which is critical for enhancer prediction tasks given the absence of consistent distinguishing signatures.
  • Why it matters Without clarity on the independent test set and negative set design, the reported accuracy and MCC cannot be interpreted. If the test set shares sequence similarity with training regions or if the negative set is trivially distinguishable, the performance metrics would be inflated and not representative of generalizable enhancer prediction.
  • Resolution test The full manuscript must describe the independent dataset construction, including source, filtering criteria, sequence similarity controls, and negative set definition. The authors should also report performance stratified by genomic context and demonstrate that the test set does not overlap with training regions at the sequence level.

  • Concern The abstract states that 26.65% of the human genome is predicted as enhancer. This is a remarkably high fraction, given that current estimates of enhancer coverage in the human genome are typically much lower, even when including candidate elements. The abstract does not explain the threshold used for genome-wide prediction, the model's false positive rate at that threshold, or how this prediction relates to the ENCODE cCRE registry used for training.

  • Resolution test The manuscript must report the precision-recall trade-off, the chosen threshold and its justification, and a comparison with existing enhancer annotations. The authors should also provide an analysis of the genomic features of the predicted regions to assess biological plausibility.

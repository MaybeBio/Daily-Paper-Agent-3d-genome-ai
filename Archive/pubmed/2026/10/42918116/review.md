## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and conclusions as presented in the abstract; no access to full text, figures, tables, or supplementary materials
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The authors report identification of a previously uncharacterized protein, C16orf87 (renamed MHAP1), as a binding partner of HDAC2 and MIER1. Using an integrative approach combining cross-linking mass spectrometry (XL-MS) with integrative structural modeling, they propose a structural model in which the C-terminal IDR of HDAC2 serves as a central interaction hub engaging the ELM2 domain of MIER1 and N- and C-terminal regions of MHAP1. They further claim this provides the first structural framework for HDAC2:MIER1:MHAP1 assembly and demonstrates the utility of XL-MS for integrative modeling of protein complexes.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; data availability statement referencing ProteomeXchange PXD082280 and MassIVE MSV000102752
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, all figures and tables, experimental methods, validation statistics for the integrative model, cross-link identification details, and any orthogonal validation experiments

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a potentially interesting application of integrative structural biology to a chromatin-associated complex containing intrinsically disordered regions. The identification of a new HDAC2/MIER1 interactor and the proposal that an IDR serves as a structural hub are conceptually appealing. However, the abstract alone provides insufficient evidence to evaluate the robustness of the identification, the quality of the XL-MS data, the validity of the integrative modeling approach, or the strength of the structural conclusions. The claims are plausible but currently not established from the provided material.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Researchers in chromatin biology and epigenetic regulation would be interested in the functional organization of HDAC2-containing complexes. Structural biologists studying intrinsically disordered proteins and those applying integrative methods (XL-MS combined with modeling) would also find the methodological demonstration relevant. The work may appeal to a specialized audience within proteomics and structural biology rather than a broad interdisciplinary readership.
- **Major strengths** The study addresses a genuinely difficult problem, namely the structural organization of IDRs within a chromatin remodeling complex, which is not tractable by conventional structural methods alone. The use of XL-MS coupled with integrative modeling is an appropriate and increasingly accepted strategy for such systems. The identification of a previously uncharacterized interactor (MHAP1) adds novelty. The data availability statement is commendable.

Without these, the structural claims cannot be assessed. Additionally, the evidence that MHAP1 is a bona fide binding partner (e.g., co-purification, stoichiometry, or affinity data) is not described. The claim that the C-terminal IDR of HDAC2 is a "key interaction hub" requires demonstration that the cross-links supporting this are unambiguous and not artifacts of nonspecific reactivity.

  - Scientific importance: The findings could be important for understanding HDAC2 complex architecture, but the significance is not yet demonstrated at a level that would warrant broad attention.
  - Interdisciplinary readership: The work is likely to appeal mainly to specialists in chromatin biology, proteomics, and integrative structural biology. The abstract does not frame the findings in a way that would attract a wider audience.
  - Technical soundness: Cannot be evaluated from the abstract. No methodological details, validation statistics, or controls are provided.
  - Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is reasonably clear but assumes familiarity with XL-MS, IDRs, and integrative modeling. The significance of the findings is not made accessible to a general scientific audience.

A supportive posture would require access to the full manuscript and validation data.

### Major Concerns

- **Concern ID** R1-M1
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Technical soundness
- **Claim pointer** The integrative structural model reveals that the C-terminal IDR of HDAC2 acts as a key interaction hub, engaging both the ELM2 domain of MIER1 and the N- and C-terminal regions of MHAP1.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract provides no information on the quality or validation of the integrative structural model. There is no mention of cross-link satisfaction, model precision, sampling convergence, or comparison to alternative models. Without such metrics, the structural conclusion cannot be evaluated.
- **Why it matters** Integrative modeling is only credible when the model is demonstrably consistent with the input data and when uncertainty is quantified. The claim that a specific IDR region acts as a hub is a strong structural statement that requires robust evidence.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript must report model validation statistics, including the fraction of cross-links satisfied by the model, the precision of the IDR placement, and an assessment of model uniqueness. If these are not provided, the structural claim should be softened or removed.

- **Concern ID** R1-M2
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Evidence quality
- **Claim pointer** We identify the previously uncharacterized protein C16orf87, which we renamed MHAP1, as a binding partner of HDAC2 and MIER1.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
- **Concern** The abstract does not describe the experimental evidence supporting the identification of MHAP1 as a binding partner. No co-purification data, affinity measurements, or interaction validation (e.g., co-immunoprecipitation, pull-down, or cellular co-localization) are mentioned.
- **Why it matters** The entire study rests on the validity of this interaction. If the identification is based solely on a single proteomics experiment without orthogonal validation, the biological relevance of the model is uncertain.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript must present clear evidence of the MHAP1:HDAC2:MIER1 interaction, ideally from multiple independent methods, and demonstrate that the interaction is specific and not an artifact of the purification or cross-linking workflow.

- **Concern ID** R1-M3
- **Severity** Major
- **Blocking** Yes
- **Axis** Claim support
- **Claim pointer** These findings provide the first structural framework for the interactions among HDAC2:MIER1:MHAP1 and demonstrate how intrinsically disordered regions can mediate assembly of a chromatin-associated deacetylase complex.
- **Evidence pointer** Abstract; location not provided
- **Concern** The claim of a "first structural framework" is strong and implies a level of confidence that cannot be assessed from the abstract. Moreover, the broader claim that IDRs can mediate assembly of such complexes is presented as a general conclusion, but the abstract provides no functional or mutational data to support the functional relevance of the proposed interactions.
- **Why it matters** Structural models without functional validation are hypothesis-generating, not conclusive. The claim that IDRs "mediate assembly" implies a functional role that requires experimental perturbation (e.g., deletion or mutation of the IDR) to establish.
- **Resolution test** The full manuscript should include functional experiments, such as IDR deletion or mutation studies, that demonstrate the necessity of the proposed hub region for complex assembly or stability. Without such data, the claim should be framed as a structural hypothesis.

## Review setup
- **Input scope** Abstract only
- **Assessment boundary** Claims and evidence presented in the abstract
- **Shared manuscript claim summary** The abstract argues that plant cis-regulatory element (CRE) function is determined by a multidimensional context (chromatin, epigenome, 3D genome, cell type), that single-cell epigenomics and CRISPR-based assays are beginning to decode this grammar, that AI platforms are transitioning from descriptive discovery to predictive engineering (outperforming natural evolution), and that a foundation model for plant regulatory grammar will enable programmable crop engineering.
- **Visible evidence base** Abstract text only; no figures, tables, methods, or references provided.
- **Missing materials affecting confidence** Full manuscript, figures, tables, methods, references, data, and code. The abstract contains no quantitative results, no experimental data, and no citations.

## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** The abstract presents a forward-looking, conceptual synthesis of plant cis-regulatory grammar with a strong emphasis on AI-driven engineering. The vision is timely and ambitious, but the abstract lacks any specific evidence, data, or mechanistic detail to support its central claims. The claim that AI platforms “outperform natural evolution” in designing synthetic CREs is stated without any supporting data or comparison. The roadmap toward a foundation model is described in aspirational terms without technical specifics. As an abstract, it reads more as a perspective or opinion piece than a review that synthesizes concrete advances. The scientific importance of the topic is clear, but the current form does not establish the case for the specific claims made.

- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Plant molecular biologists, synthetic biologists, and computational biologists interested in gene regulation and crop engineering. The vision of AI-driven predictive design of CREs would also appeal to researchers in plant biotechnology and agricultural genomics. However, the abstract does not provide enough detail to distinguish this work from existing reviews or perspectives on the same topic.

- **Major strengths** 1. The topic is of high current interest and importance for crop engineering. 2. The conceptual framing of CRE function as emerging from a multidimensional context is well articulated. 3. The integration of single-cell epigenomics, CRISPR, and AI is a timely synthesis.

- **Major Concerns**
    - **Concern ID** R1-M1
    - **Severity** Major
    - **Blocking** Yes
    - **Axis** Evidence sufficiency
    - **Claim pointer** “AI platforms are catalyzing an ongoing transition from descriptive discovery to predictive engineering, wherein these platforms outperform natural evolution in designing synthetic CREs.”
    - **Evidence pointer** Abstract only; no data, figures, or references provided.
    - **Concern** The abstract makes a strong, quantitative claim that AI platforms “outperform natural evolution” in designing synthetic CREs. No evidence, examples, benchmarks, or comparisons are provided to support this claim. The abstract does not specify which AI platforms, what metrics were used, or what the baseline for “natural evolution” is.
    - **Why it matters** This is the most striking and potentially novel claim in the abstract. Without any supporting evidence, the claim is unsubstantiated and undermines the credibility of the entire review. If this claim is central to the manuscript’s thesis, it must be rigorously supported with data or at least cited from primary literature.
    - **Resolution test** Provide specific examples, quantitative comparisons (e.g., activity levels, specificity, or evolutionary rates), and citations to primary studies that demonstrate AI-designed CREs outperforming naturally evolved ones. If the claim is aspirational rather than established, it should be reframed as a hypothesis or future direction.

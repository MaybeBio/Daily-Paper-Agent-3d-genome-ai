## Reviewer
- **Overall assessment** This abstract presents a broad, forward-looking perspective on plant cis-regulatory grammar, integrating recent technological advances and proposing a roadmap toward AI-driven predictive engineering. The topic is timely and of potential interest to a wide readership in plant biology, genomics, and synthetic biology. However, the abstract is largely programmatic and aspirational in tone, with no specific evidence, examples, or critical evaluation provided. The claims are plausible but currently unverifiable from the supplied material. The review would benefit from concrete illustrations of the proposed principles, a critical assessment of current limitations, and a clearer articulation of what is genuinely novel versus what synthesizes existing knowledge.
- **Who would be interested in the results, and why** Plant molecular biologists, epigenomics researchers, computational biologists, and crop engineers would be interested in this review as a synthesis of current approaches to understanding cis-regulatory grammar and a roadmap for future AI-driven design. The proposed foundation model concept may also appeal to researchers working at the interface of machine learning and genomics. The agricultural applications could attract translational scientists focused on crop improvement.
- **Major strengths** The abstract identifies a timely and important problem, namely the context-dependent nature of CRE function. It successfully integrates multiple emerging technologies into a coherent framework. The proposed transition from descriptive to predictive engineering is a compelling framing. The roadmap toward a foundation model is forward-looking and potentially influential.
- **Major Concerns** The abstract makes strong claims about the state of the field and the capabilities of AI platforms without providing any supporting evidence or examples. The claim that AI platforms "outperform natural evolution in designing synthetic CREs" is particularly strong and requires substantiation. The abstract does not acknowledge limitations, controversies, or alternative viewpoints. The scope of the review is unclear, including which organisms, which types of CREs, and which regulatory layers are covered in depth.
- **Minor Comments** The abstract would benefit from a clearer statement of the review's scope and structure. The term "grammar" is used metaphorically but not defined operationally. The relationship between the proposed foundation model and existing models in other domains is not addressed. The abstract could more explicitly distinguish between established knowledge, recent advances, and speculative future directions.
- **Technical failings that need to be addressed before the case is established** None can be identified from the abstract alone, as no technical details or data are presented. However, the strong claim about AI outperforming natural evolution requires empirical support or at least citation of specific studies.
- **Assessment against Nature-style criteria** Originality: The framing of CRE function as multidimensional context and the proposal of a foundation model are somewhat original, though similar ideas are emerging in the field. Scientific importance: The topic is important, but the abstract does not demonstrate that the review provides new insights beyond existing syntheses. Interdisciplinary readership: The abstract could appeal to multiple communities, but the language is somewhat jargon-heavy. Technical soundness: Cannot be assessed from the abstract; no methods or data are presented. Readability for nonspecialists: The abstract is accessible but assumes familiarity with several advanced concepts.
- **Recommendation posture** Currently not established from the provided evidence. The abstract is promising but too programmatic to evaluate the quality of the synthesis or the validity of the claims. A full manuscript with concrete examples, critical analysis, and appropriate citations would be needed to assess whether the case is made.
